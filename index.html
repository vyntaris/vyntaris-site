What it needs

  • A 64-bit PC (x86-64) that starts in UEFI mode
  • 30 GB on the disk and 4 GB of memory
  • The internet while installing, by cable or Wi-Fi
  • A USB stick of 4 GB or more

  1. 1. Check what you downloaded

    With SHA256SUMS, SHA256SUMS.sig and vyntaris.gpg beside it:

    sha256sum --ignore-missing -c SHA256SUMS
    gpg --import vyntaris.gpg && gpg --verify SHA256SUMS.sig SHA256SUMS

  2. 2. Put it on a USB stick

    Copy it to a Ventoy stick, or write it directly. This erases the stick; sdX is the stick.

    sudo dd if=vyntaris-x86_64.iso of=/dev/sdX bs=4M status=progress

  3. 3. Start from it

    Turn Secure Boot off and start from the stick. The installer asks for the internet, your language and keyboard, the time zone, who you are and which disk.

Before you install

Secure Boot has to be off to start

Once Vyntaris is installed, vyntaris-secure-boot signs it with keys of your own, and Secure Boot can go back on.

It can sit next to Windows

The installer can use a disk's free space, or one partition of 30 GB or more, and leave Windows as it is. Make room in Windows first (Disk Management, Shrink volume), and with BitLocker keep its recovery key at hand.

Copy your files somewhere first

“Whole disk” erases everything on it. Snapshots keep the system, never your files.

NVIDIA, AMD and Intel

Their drivers install by themselves. On a laptop with two graphics cards, the NVIDIA one sleeps until a game or prime-run wakes it.

Updates are rolling, from Arch

They come when they're ready, from Arch and from Vyntaris, with a snapshot taken before each one. There are no big upgrades to wait for.

Something wrong?

See Help, or tell us on the issue tracker. Dr. Help gathers what the system says, without your names and addresses, and shows it to you first.
